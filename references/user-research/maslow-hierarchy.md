# Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs

## Core Concept
5-level need hierarchy: Physiological → Safety → Love/Belonging → Esteem → Self-Actualization. Users are motivated by their current dominant unmet need. Products that address the right need level resonate more deeply.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- User need mining
- Product positioning
- Marketing appeal design
- Upgrade path planning

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- Ladder-literal product reasoning — assuming users must satisfy lower needs before your higher-level appeal lands; needs don't march in strict order, so treat the hierarchy as a lens, not a sequence
- Feature prioritization — mapping backlog items to pyramid levels is pseudo-rigor; use **Kano Model** for feature-level decisions
- Single-level positioning — real products serve several levels at once (safety plus belonging); map the mix, not one rung
- Cross-cultural universality claims — need salience varies by culture and context; validate the dominant need locally before building appeals

## Key Steps
1. Identify your target users' dominant need level through interviews/behavior analysis
2. Map your product's value proposition to the need hierarchy
3. Assess: does your product address the right need level for your target users?
4. Design marketing appeals that resonate with the dominant need level
5. Plan upgrade paths: as lower needs are met, users seek higher-level fulfillment

## Output Template

```
Target users: [segment]  Evidence: [# interviews / behavior data]
Dominant need level: [Physiological / Safety / Belonging / Esteem / Self-Actualization] — salience: [strong/mixed/weak]
Secondary needs in play: [levels + evidence]
Product mapping: [which need(s) our value proposition actually serves]
Fit assessment: [does the dominant need match what we serve? what's the gap?]
Appeal design: [message tuned to the dominant need — don't claim a level we don't deliver]
Upgrade hypothesis: [as X is met, users seek Y — and what product change serves it]
```

## Failure Modes
- Ladder literalism: treating the hierarchy as a strict sequence users climb → use it to diagnose need salience; people pursue multiple levels at once and skip levels
- Single-rung positioning: pinning the product to "self-actualization" while ignoring the security expectations that gate adoption → map the full mix of needs the product touches
- Culture-free universality: applying Western-individualist salience everywhere → validate which needs dominate in this market's context before crafting appeals
- Feature-level misapplication: rating backlog items by pyramid level → Maslow informs positioning and messaging, not feature ranking; that's **Kano Model**'s job

## Evidence Strength
Contested — as a strict motivational sequence the hierarchy has weak empirical support: later research shows needs pursued in parallel with order varying by person and culture. It remains a useful heuristic lens for need salience and messaging design, but do not build product logic on the assumption that needs are satisfied in order.

## Source
Abraham Maslow, *A Theory of Human Motivation* (1943).
