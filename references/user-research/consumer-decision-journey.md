# Consumer Decision Journey

## Core Concept
Non-linear decision journey: Consider → Evaluate → Purchase → Experience → Advocate. Unlike the traditional funnel, consumers loop back and forth between stages. Map touchpoints at each stage to optimize the journey.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Journey optimization
- Touchpoint design
- Churn analysis
- Loyalty loop design

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- B2B committee buying — the CDJ models an individual consumer loop; multi-stakeholder purchases need **B2B Buying Center Mapping** (Go-to-Market)
- Service-stage redesign (onboarding, support, renewal) — the **Customer Journey Map** covers the full lifecycle with stage owners; the CDJ is sharpest on the pre-purchase loop and advocacy
- Categories where the loop doesn't cycle — one-shot or infrequent purchases may have no loyalty loop to optimize
- Replacing measurement with mapping — the journey is a hypothesis; stage-level churn data decides, not the diagram

## Key Steps
1. Map the 5 stages for your product category
2. For each stage, identify: touchpoints, decision drivers, emotional state, drop-off risk
3. Measure: experience score per stage, churn rate per stage
4. Identify the weakest stage (highest churn or lowest satisfaction)
5. Design interventions for the weakest stage
6. Monitor the loyalty loop: do advocates feed back into Consider?

## Output Template

```
Category: [product category] — confirm the 5 stages apply: [evidence]
Stage: [Consider / Evaluate / Purchase / Experience / Advocate]
  Touchpoints: [ranked by influence]
  Decision drivers: [what moves users forward]
  Drop-off risk: [where and why users stall or exit]
  Experience score: [x]  Churn rate: [%]
Weakest stage: [name] — evidence: [data]
Intervention: [what changes, owned by whom, measured by]
Loyalty loop: [does advocacy feed Consider? evidence: [data]]
```

## Failure Modes
- Funnel relapse: drawing the journey as one-way arrows → keep the loops explicit; the model's entire value is that evaluation and advocacy feed back into consideration
- Mapped but unmeasured: stages charted from internal opinion → ground each stage in behavioral or survey data; unmeasured stages hide the real weakest link
- Optimizing the measurable stage: fixing checkout because it's instrumented while consideration leaks silently → rank stages by drop-off and satisfaction evidence, then fix the weakest
- Assumed advocacy: treating the loyalty loop as automatic → measure whether advocates actually feed new consideration; in most categories the loop needs engineering, not hope

## Evidence Strength
Mixed — the CDJ reframed funnel thinking and is widely adopted in consumer marketing, but its empirical basis is McKinsey's own category research rather than a broad independent literature; stage universality and loyalty-loop strength vary by category, and low-involvement purchases often skip the full loop. Use it as a mapping lens validated per category, not a universal sequence.

## Source
McKinsey & Company, *The Consumer Decision Journey* (2009).
