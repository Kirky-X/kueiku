# Six Thinking Hats

## Core Concept
6 thinking modes that teams switch between simultaneously: White (facts/data), Red (emotions/intuition), Black (caution/risks), Yellow (optimism/benefits), Green (creativity/alternatives), Blue (process/facilitation). Forces the team to look at a problem from the same angle at the same time.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Team review and evaluation
- Comprehensive solution assessment
- Breaking discussion deadlocks

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- Solo work or 1-2 people — parallel thinking pays off when enough perspectives collide; for tiny groups the rotation is ceremony — structure the analysis with **MECE + Pyramid Principle** instead
- Decisions needing weighted trade-offs — hats sequence perspectives but never score them; for ranking, use **Decision Matrix**
- Time-critical situations — six rounds of full-team attention is expensive; in fast-moving response use **OODA Loop**
- A genuinely simple call — running the full hat cycle on an obvious decision buries it in process

## Key Steps
1. **Blue Hat** (facilitator): define the problem, set the agenda
2. **White Hat**: gather and present facts and data
3. **Red Hat**: share emotions and intuitions (no justification needed)
4. **Yellow Hat**: identify benefits and best-case scenarios
5. **Black Hat**: identify risks, cautions, potential problems
6. **Green Hat**: generate creative alternatives and new ideas
7. **Blue Hat** (facilitator): summarize, decide next steps

## Output Template

```
Focus: [the question under the hats]
Blue (process): [agenda, hat sequence, timebox per round]
White: [known facts and data; gaps named]
Red: [gut reactions, one sentence each, no justification]
Yellow: [benefits, value, why it could work]
Black: [risks, weaknesses, what could fail]
Green: [alternatives; ways to fix the black-hat findings]
Blue (summary): [decision / next step / what gets re-examined and when]
```

## Failure Modes
- Hats as theater: people label their usual stance ("my black hat says...") without shifting attention → enforce one hat per round with a visible mode switch: facilitator names the hat, everyone contributes under it, cross-hat remarks ruled out
- Red hat skipped or argued: feelings are judged the moment they are voiced → the red round gets no justification and no rebuttal; the facilitator protects it
- Black hat first: criticism arrives before ideas mature and kills the green round prematurely → yellow precedes black when assessing anything new; if risks dominated round one, rerun green before deciding
- Blue hat absent: no agenda and no summary, so hats become freeform discussion → the facilitator opens by stating the focus and closes with a decision or a next step

## Evidence Strength
Practitioner consensus — six-hat sessions are widely adopted in facilitation and corporate training and are credited with reducing adversarial discussion; causal evidence on decision quality is thin: the claim that separating thinking modes improves group cognition is plausible and consistent with perspective-taking research, but controlled studies of the method itself are scarce, and much of the supporting literature is de Bono's own.

## Source
Edward de Bono, *Six Thinking Hats* (1985).
