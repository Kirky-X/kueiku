# A-Player Density

## Core Concept
A-players hire A-players; B-players hire C-players. Talent density determines team ceiling. Once B/C players enter, the team's capability declines irreversibly unless addressed. Maintain high density through rigorous hiring, performance management, and sometimes termination.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Hiring decisions
- Team building
- Performance management

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- The constraint is team structure or boundaries, not individual quality → **Team Topologies** — no hiring decision fixes a dependency bottleneck or an overloaded scope
- The team is too small to have a hiring or exit lever — with five people, "raise the bar" changes nothing until someone leaves; use the behavior standard as a development plan instead of a ranking
- Competence is defined by credentials or domain standards (clinical, legal, safety-critical engineering) — "A-player" is undefined without a competency rubric, so the audit becomes taste dressed as rigor
- The gap is a missing skill or missing tooling, not missing capability → diagnose the capability gap first; a rating without a development or resourcing answer is a verdict, not a fix

## Key Steps
1. Define A-player behavior standards (not just title/credentials)
2. Audit current team: who are A/B/C players by behavior?
3. For B players: can they be developed to A? Set timeline.
4. For C players: honest conversation about fit; transition out if needed
5. Raise hiring bar: only hire people you'd be excited to work with
6. Monitor density continuously; address declines early

## Output Template

```
A-player behavior standard: [3-5 observable behaviors + where the evidence comes from]
Team audit: [name | A/B/C by behavior | written evidence | trajectory (improving/stagnant/declining)]
Development cases: [B player | missing A behavior | plan + owner + deadline]
Exit decisions: [C player | honest-conversation date | transition plan | knowledge-transfer risk]
Hiring bar: [required behaviors vs. required credentials | what the bar now rejects]
Density: current [%A / %B / %C] → target [%A / %B / %C] by [date]
```

## Failure Modes
- Labeling by vibe: A/B/C assigned from title, seniority, or instinct → publish the behavior standard first and require written evidence per rating; a rating without evidence is a guess with a letter attached
- A-player worship: everyone must be an A, so strong B-players get no legitimate place → grade the role, not the person, and give B-players a named path to A
- A bar with no roles behind it: raising the standard while the hiring pipeline is frozen → pair the bar with an actual hiring plan; a bar nobody can clear is a rejection letter, not a strategy
- Termination without a documented standard: the C-list becomes legally and morally indefensible → document standards and evidence first, run the honest conversation, and prepare the transition and handover
- Density as a one-time score: measured at a reorg, then never again → track it on a fixed cadence; density decays quietly through ordinary attrition

## Evidence Strength
Practitioner consensus — the tiering originates in Netflix's culture documentation and is widely adopted as hiring and performance language, and its core claim (clear standards plus selective hiring raise the ceiling of a team) is plausible and consistent with mainstream talent practice, but it is a business artifact rather than a validated instrument: no controlled evidence supports the specific tier thresholds, the "A-player" definition is usually left unstated until after ratings are made, and the sharpest documented failure (a bar so high it starves hiring or freezes internal mobility) comes from practitioner critique rather than from measurement.
