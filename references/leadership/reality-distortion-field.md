# Reality Distortion Field

## Core Concept
Through conviction and expression, make the team believe the impossible is possible. Not about lying — it's about setting audacious goals and creating unwavering belief that they can be achieved. Must have exit conditions to prevent toxicity.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Pushing teams past self-imposed limits
- Selling vision (investors, customers, team)
- Rally speeches and mobilization

⚠️ **Use with caution**
- Must have exit conditions (when to acknowledge reality)
- Not sustainable as a long-term management style
- Requires genuine belief from the leader

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- Safety-critical, regulatory, or factual claims — the method manufactures conviction, not evidence; a belief amplified forty times is still wrong when the underlying claim is false
- Situations where buy-in must be genuine and earned — pressure applied instead of shared reasoning produces compliance in the room and quiet resistance after it
- A team that has already been burned by an over-promised target — the credibility cost of the next miss exceeds the motivational gain; rebuild a delivery record first
- Problems whose cause is structural rather than motivational — missing tooling, unclear ownership, or misaligned incentives → **Change Management** or **Team Topologies**

## Key Steps
1. Set an audacious but not delusional goal
2. Articulate a compelling vision of the future
3. Demonstrate unwavering personal conviction
4. Create urgency and momentum
5. Define exit conditions: when to pivot or acknowledge failure
6. Follow through with action — credibility requires delivery

## Output Template

```
Audacious goal: [one outcome + deadline + what "done" means]
Belief gap: [where the team is now | what they would have to believe]
Reality check: [what must be true for the goal to be reachable | earliest evidence of it]
Vision narrative: [future state, concrete enough to be checkable]
Exit conditions: [metric or date | if it is not met, the pivot or the acknowledgment]
Delivery commitments: [first 30 / 60 / 90-day deliverable | owner | date]
Belief check: [the observable behavior that shows the team believes it, not says it]
```

## Failure Modes
- Conviction without delivery: the goal stays "believed" while nothing moves → date and publish the first 30-day deliverable; belief with no delivery converts into cynicism faster than no belief
- Goal drift into delusion: the target slides each time a milestone is missed → fix the deadline and the exit condition before the first miss; a target that moves can never fail, so it can never be won
- Exit conditions that never trigger: the leader is committed, so no evidence ever counts as failure → name a party outside the commitment to adjudicate the exit condition
- Belief substituted for capability: conviction replaces the skills, budget, or dependency the goal needs → check feasibility (people, money, dependencies) separately from belief; the two fail independently
- Toxic residue: fear-driven performance outlasts the goal → pair with **Change Management**; a rally leaves an organization to live in afterwards

## Evidence Strength
Practitioner consensus — this is biography-derived lore, not a tested method. The term comes from Apple employees describing Steve Jobs, the supporting account is retrospective journalism and anecdote, and there is no controlled evidence that transferring conviction produces better outcomes than ordinary motivation or better planning. Its real value is as a description of a recognizable leadership behavior and as a diagnostic for teams that have stopped attempting hard things; the well-documented failure modes (delusion, moved goalposts, morale damage after a miss) are exactly why the exit conditions are not optional decoration.
