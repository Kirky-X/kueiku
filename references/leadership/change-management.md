# Change Management

## Core Concept
Systematically drive organizational change using Kotter's 8-step process + ADKAR model. Kotter focuses on organizational momentum; ADKAR focuses on individual transition. Use both for comprehensive change.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Organizational transformation
- Cultural change
- Large-scale behavior change

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- The change is one person's or one team's skill gap — an eight-step organizational process applied to a single behavior wastes months → coach it directly
- A live crisis or incident — the sequence assumes time for coalition-building; contain the damage first, then change
- The target state itself is contested — without agreement on the vision, the communication steps produce theater; settle the strategy question before running the sequence
- The real blocker is team structure or handoff friction → **Team Topologies** — restructure the boundaries first, or instead of, running the sequence

## Key Steps (Kotter 8-Step)
1. Create urgency — why change now?
2. Build a guiding coalition — assemble influential change champions
3. Form a strategic vision — clear, compelling future state
4. Enlist a volunteer army — communicate broadly, get buy-in
5. Enable action by removing barriers — remove obstacles
6. Generate short-term wins — celebrate early victories
7. Sustain acceleration — keep momentum, don't declare victory too early
8. Institute change — anchor in culture

## Key Steps (ADKAR per individual)
1. **A**wareness — of the need for change
2. **D**esire — to participate and support
3. **K**nowledge — on how to change
4. **A**bility — to implement new skills/behaviors
5. **R**einforcement — to sustain the change

## Output Template

```
Change objective: [what must be true after, by when, and for whom]
Urgency case: [why now — the cost of the status quo]
Guiding coalition: [name | role | influence | what they must do]
Vision statement: [one sentence describing the future state]
Stakeholder ADKAR: [name | current state per A/D/K/A/R | blocker | owner + date]
Barriers to action: [structural blocker | owner | removal action]
Short-term wins (first 90 days): [deliverable | metric | date]
Reinforcement: [mechanism | cadence | owner | what happens if the old behavior returns]
Change review: [checkpoint dates | evidence expected at each checkpoint]
```

## Failure Modes
- Announcement mistaken for adoption: the org hears the new direction and behaves as before → measure behavior at the checkpoints, not announcement reach
- Coalition of the converted: the guiding coalition is made of people who already agreed, so skeptics never engage it → recruit skeptics into the coalition; its job is dissent, not cheerleading
- Symbolic short-term wins: early wins are picked for visibility, not for practicing the new behavior → tie each win to the behavior that must change
- Reinforcement never lands: old incentives, OKRs, and metrics still reward the old way → change the incentive structure before declaring victory
- Kotter and ADKAR desynchronized: the org narrative advances while individual transitions stall → track the per-person ADKAR column, not the org-level slide

## Evidence Strength
Practitioner consensus — Kotter's sequence and ADKAR are among the most widely used change frameworks in large organizations, and the ADKAR diagnostic has survey-based support for the claim that individual transition states, not communication volume, predict adoption. The causal evidence is thin: success is mostly self-reported practitioner assessment, the frameworks are rarely compared against simpler well-executed interventions, and the documented failure mode of most failed programs is not the absence of a framework but the absence of follow-through on the last two steps.
