# VRIO Framework

## Core Concept
4-question resource-based view: Is it Valuable? Rare? Costly to Imitate? Organized to capture value? Determines sustainable competitive advantage.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Resource/capability assessment, sustainable competitive advantage identification

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- Listing generic resources ("brand", "team") instead of specific ones — VRIO on vague nouns produces vague verdicts
- Fast-moving advantage environments where "sustainable" means months — ask "how long is imitation costly" instead
- Assessing an early-stage company's hypothetical resources — there is rarely enough evidence to answer O honestly

## Key Steps
1. List key resources and capabilities
2. For each, answer 4 questions: Valuable? Rare? Costly to Imitate? Organized?
3. Classification:
   - No to V = Competitive Disadvantage
   - V yes, R no = Competitive Parity
   - V+R yes, I no = Temporary Advantage
   - V+R+I yes, O no = Unused Advantage
   - All yes = Sustainable Competitive Advantage

## Output Template

```
| Resource / capability   | V? | R? | I (costly to imitate)? | O (organized)? | Verdict                 | Action              |
| ----------------------- | -- | -- | ---------------------- | -------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------- |
| [proprietary usage data]| Y  | Y  | Y (10-yr accumulation) | N (no feedback loop using it) | Unused Advantage | [build the loop]    |
| [exclusive channel deal]| Y  | Y  | N (replicable in 1 yr) | Y              | Temporary Advantage     | [convert to durable: ...] |
| [generic SaaS stack]    | Y  | N  | N                      | Y              | Competitive Parity      | [table stakes]      |
| [legacy process]        | N  | —  | —                      | —              | Competitive Disadvantage| [retire]            |

Portfolio verdict: durable advantage rests on [item]; imitation halflife estimate: [time]
```

## Failure Modes
- Answering V/R/I from inside the company: "our data is unique" without market evidence → each Yes needs an external proof point (competitor equivalent named and compared)
- O as an afterthought: the unused-advantage row is where most real firms live — value exists but structure doesn't capture it → treat O-gaps as first-class findings
- Sustainability inflation: "costly to imitate" asserted without naming the cost driver (path dependency, causal ambiguity, complexity) → no driver, no I

## Evidence Strength
Practitioner consensus with strong academic roots — the resource-based view is a central strategy theory, but applying it reliably is hard: imitation costs are unobservable ex ante, and the framework's verdicts degrade to hypotheses without competitor data.

## Source
Jay Barney (1991); resource-based view of the firm.
