# Benchmarking

## Core Concept
Identify gaps by comparing against industry/cross-industry best practices.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Performance improvement, process improvement, best practice learning

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- No comparable peer data exists and none can be constructed — the gap numbers would be fiction
- The goal is differentiation, not parity — benchmarking converges you toward the average of best practice, which is the opposite of standing out
- The process being compared is entangled with a deliberately different strategy (cost vs premium) — copying "best" breaks the model

## Key Steps
1. Identify what to benchmark (process, metric, practice)
2. Select benchmark targets (industry leaders + cross-industry best)
3. Gather data (at least 2 independent sources)
4. Analyze gaps: where are you behind? By how much?
5. Develop improvement plan based on best practices

## Output Template

```
Benchmark subject: [process/metric]
Comparison set: [peer A, peer B, cross-industry C]

| Metric       | Us    | Peer A | Best-in-class | Gap    | Root driver of the gap     |
| ------------ | ----- | ------ | ------------- | ------ | -------------------------- |
| [throughput] | 100   | 150    | 180           | -44%   | [batching / automation]    |
| [cost/unit]  | 9.0   | 7.5    | 7.0           | +29%   | [vendor contract]          |

Adoption plan: [practice] from [target] — adapted how for our context: [note]; owner: [x]; target: [metric by date]
Not adopting: [practice] — [why it conflicts with our strategy]
```

## Failure Modes
- Metric worship: hitting the benchmark number while missing the practice that produces it → benchmark the mechanism, not just the KPI
- Context blindness: adopting a practice that depends on scale, regulation, or culture you don't have → adapt before adopting, state what was changed
- Benchmarking the laggards (only peers who share data) → triangulate at least 2 independent sources per data point

## Evidence Strength
Practitioner consensus — gap analysis and target setting are uncontroversial mechanics; claims that benchmarking per se drives performance gains are weaker (firms that benchmark often already differ in management quality, a known confounder).
