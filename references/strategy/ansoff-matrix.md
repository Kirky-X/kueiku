# Ansoff Matrix

## Core Concept
Existing/new markets × existing/new products 4-quadrant growth strategy.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Growth direction selection, market expansion, diversification decisions

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- The growth option is already forced (no viable "existing market" path) — the matrix adds vocabulary, not a decision
- "Existing vs new" cannot be defined crisply for your product (e.g. adjacent segments) — boundary debates replace analysis
- Growth requires sequencing many moves over time — use a roadmap instead of a single quadrant pick

## Key Steps
1. Map current products to markets
2. Identify growth options: Market Penetration (existing/existing), Market Development (existing/new market), Product Development (new/existing market), Diversification (new/new)
3. Assess risk per option (diversification = highest risk)
4. Select growth strategy based on risk appetite and capability

## Output Template

```
Growth options assessed:
  Market Penetration  [products: A, B → market: current]  risk: low   — [feasibility note]
  Product Development [new product → current market]       risk: med   — [capability gap]
  Market Development  [product A → new segment/geography]  risk: med   — [entry barrier]
  Diversification     [new/new]                            risk: high  — [why consider at all]

Selected: [option] — risk appetite: [x]; capability evidence: [why we can execute]
Deferred: [option] — [revisit condition]
```

## Failure Modes
- Boundary fuzz: a "new" market that is really the existing market relabeled → write explicit criteria for what counts as new before plotting
- Risk ranked but never weighed against capability → pair the selected quadrant with a capability check (VRIO)
- Diversification chosen for growth optics alone → require an explicit answer to "why us, why this market"

## Evidence Strength
Practitioner consensus — the risk ordering is directionally supported by decades of diversification research (related diversification tends to outperform unrelated), but the matrix itself is a vocabulary, not a predictor of outcomes.

## Source
Igor Ansoff (1957); classic growth strategy framework.
