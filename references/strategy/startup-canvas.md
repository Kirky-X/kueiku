# Startup Canvas

## Core Concept
9-block strategy + Cost Structure + Revenue Streams, separating strategy from business model.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Early-stage startups needing to answer strategy and monetization simultaneously

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- Pure strategy work with monetization already settled — half the canvas is dead weight; use Product Strategy Canvas
- Pre-problem-validation — both halves will encode unvalidated guesses; run discovery interviews first
- Complex multi-side businesses — strategy/model per side, not one averaged canvas

## Key Steps
1. Define strategy blocks (customer, problem, solution, value prop)
2. Define business model blocks (cost structure, revenue streams)
3. Validate: does the strategy support the business model?
4. Iterate strategy and business model independently

## Output Template

```
STRATEGY HALF
  Customer    | [who, ranked by urgency]
  Problem     | [top 1-2 pains, with cost of the pain]
  Solution    | [1-3 capabilities, no more]
  Value Prop  | [from → to instead of alternative]

BUSINESS MODEL HALF
  Revenue Streams | [model + who pays + price]
  Cost Structure  | [what dominates: build / serve / acquire]
  Fit check: gross margin per [customer] ≈ [price − serve cost] → [positive/underwater]

Coherence questions:
  Does the strategy segment match who can pay? [answer]
  Does the solution's cost shape fit the revenue model? [answer]
Weakest link right now: [block] → next test: [experiment by date]
```

## Failure Modes
- Halves filled by different people who never reconcile: the strategy targets non-payers, the model prices what strategy can't deliver → always run the two coherence questions in joint session
- Strategy half copied from an incumbent, model half from another — the mashup has no owner economics → check margin per customer before iterating either half
- Iterate independently = iterate never: "independent" blocks go stale at different rates → version the canvas, date each half's last change

## Evidence Strength
Practitioner consensus — a framing device combining standard strategy blocks with business-model blocks; the separation is useful discipline, not an empirically validated model, and its output is only as good as the discovery behind each half.

## Source
Startup strategy methodology; separating strategic choices from business model design.
