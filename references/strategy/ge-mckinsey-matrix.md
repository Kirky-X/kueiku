# GE-McKinsey Matrix

## Core Concept
Industry attractiveness × business strength 9-cell matrix. An upgraded BCG matrix for diversified portfolio management.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Diversified business portfolio management, SBU resource allocation

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- Fewer than ~3 business units — the 9-cell machinery costs more than it returns; use a simple decision matrix
- Attractiveness or strength cannot be scored with defensible criteria — composite scores hide the guesses
- Capital allocation is not actually on the table (units are strategic commitments) — the matrix implies divestment options that don't exist

## Key Steps
1. List all SBUs (Strategic Business Units)
2. Assess Industry Attractiveness (High/Medium/Low) for each
3. Assess Business Strength (High/Medium/Low) for each
4. Plot on 3×3 matrix
5. Strategy: Invest/Grow (top-left), Selectivity (diagonal), Harvest/Divest (bottom-right)

## Output Template

```
Scoring criteria (declare before scoring):
  Industry attractiveness = [market size 30% / growth 30% / margin 20% / regulation 20%]
  Business strength       = [share 30% / margin vs peers 30% / brand 20% / capabilities 20%]

| SBU | Attractiveness | Strength | Cell              | Action                | Capital shift |
| --- | -------------- | -------- | ----------------- | --------------------- | ------------- |
| [A] | 7.2 High       | 7.8 High | Invest/Grow       | [double down]         | +[x]          |
| [B] | 5.0 Medium     | 5.5 Med  | Selectivity       | [invest in wins only] | 0             |
| [C] | 2.8 Low        | 3.1 Low  | Harvest/Divest    | [exit by date]        | -[x]          |

Rebalance summary: [net reallocation and rationale]
```

## Failure Modes
- Composite-score laundering: two units with identical 5.0 scores built from completely different criteria mixes → publish the criteria weights and per-factor scores
- Central overreach: corporate picks winners for markets only the unit team knows → score jointly, let the unit challenge attractiveness inputs
- Harbor/Divest autopilot: bottom-right cells exit by default, killing turnaround candidates → require a stated turnaround thesis before harvesting

## Evidence Strength
Practitioner consensus — the multi-factor scoring fixes BCG's two-proxy rigidity and is standard in corporate strategy; but like all portfolio matrices, its output quality is bounded by the scoring criteria, and there is no evidence the 9-cell grid beats simpler allocations when criteria are weak.

## Source
McKinsey & Company / GE (1970s); multi-portfolio analysis.
