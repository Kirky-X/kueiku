# Scenario Planning

## Core Concept
Under deep uncertainty — where outcomes depend on forces you cannot forecast — stop betting on one predicted future. Construct 3-4 plausible, internally consistent, structurally different futures; test the strategy against every one; act on what survives across all of them (robust moves), place hedges where scenarios diverge, and monitor signposts that tell you which future is arriving.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Multi-year strategy amid regulatory/market/technology upheaval
- Large irreversible bets (capacity, platform, market entry) with long payback
- Challenging a single-point forecast a plan silently depends on

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- Near-term planning in a stable trend — forecasting plus Product Life Cycle is sharper and cheaper
- Narrow, quantifiable uncertainty (one number, estimable) → Probabilistic Reasoning (Decision Making) — scenarios are for structural, not statistical, uncertainty
- A specific interactive contest against identifiable rivals → Game Theory
- No willingness to act differently per scenario — the exercise becomes a document, not a decision

## Key Steps
1. Define the focal question and time horizon ("Should we build the second factory by 2029?")
2. List driving forces; rank by impact × uncertainty; discard the predetermined (aging population) and the trivial
3. Pick the two highest-impact uncertainties as axes; the 2×2 yields four candidate scenarios
4. Flesh out each scenario: internally consistent narrative, named winners/losers, observable signposts
5. Stress-test the current strategy in each: win / survive / die
6. Choose the response set: no-regret moves (pay off in all four), big bets (pay off in one), hedges (cheap options on divergence)
7. Set signpost monitoring with owners and triggers to re-plan

## Output Template

```
Focal question: [decision + horizon]
Driving forces ranked: [impact × uncertainty, predetermined stripped]
Scenario 1..4: [name + narrative + signposts: [observable events]]
Strategy robustness matrix: [strategy × scenario → win/survive/die]
Response set: no-regret: [moves]; big bets: [moves + the scenario they ride]; hedges: [options + cost]
Signpost dashboard: [event → owner → trigger action]
Confidence: [what would invalidate the driver ranking]
```

## Failure Modes
- Rosy-vs-grim collapse: the four scenarios differ only by mood, not structure → force orthogonal axes and check that each scenario's winners differ
- Forecast in disguise: probabilities assigned to scenarios and one picked → scenarios are instruments for robustness, not weighted predictions
- No decision changed: the strategy survives all four scenarios unchanged → either the strategies tested are too coarse, or say openly what the exercise failed to move
- Signpost-free plan: scenarios written, monitoring never set — the plan is blind within a quarter
- Scenario explosion: six scenarios that nobody can hold in mind → 3-4, structurally distinct, named memorably

## Evidence Strength
Practitioner consensus — Shell's scenario work anticipating the 1973 oil shock is the canonical case, and scenario methods are standard in energy, defense, and sovereign planning; as a forecasting-improvement device the evidence is mixed (scenarios improve preparedness and assumption discipline, not point-forecast accuracy).
