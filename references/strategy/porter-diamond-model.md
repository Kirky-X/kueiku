# Porter Diamond Model

## Core Concept
National competitive advantage: Factor Conditions, Demand Conditions, Related Industries, Firm Strategy/Rivalry + 2 variables (Government, Chance).

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Cross-border site selection, industrial policy, national competitiveness analysis

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- Firm-level strategy inside one market — the diamond explains why locations excel, not why your company wins
- Digital/remote industries where geography barely binds (talent and customers are global) — factor conditions weaken as constraints
- Short-horizon decisions — diamond advantages take decades to build or erode

## Key Steps
1. Assess each of the 4 main factors for the target country/industry
2. Assess Government and Chance variables
3. Identify competitive advantages from factor combinations
4. Make location/investment decisions based on diamond analysis

## Output Template

```
Target: [country] × [industry]

Factor Conditions        — [specialized talent, infrastructure, capital: strength + evidence]
Demand Conditions        — [sophistication of local buyers: what they demand earlier/harder]
Related/Supporting Ind.  — [supplier and complementary clusters nearby]
Firm Strategy & Rivalry  — [intensity of local competition: sharpens or bleeds]
Government               — [policy levers and risks: incentives, protection, instability]
Chance                   — [exogenous events that could re-rank the above]

Verdict: locate/expand here because [combination advantage]; hedge against [top 2 risks]
Revisit trigger: [election / regulation / demand shift]
```

## Failure Modes
- Checklist flattening: four boxes ticked, no interaction analyzed — the model's point is that advantages reinforce each other (skilled suppliers + demanding buyers + rivalry) → write at least one reinforcing loop per verdict
- Government read as permanent: incentives get renegotiated → separate structural factors from policy-dependent ones in the verdict
- National averages hiding regional variance — diamond strength is cluster-specific (a country can be strong in autos, weak in software) → score the cluster, not the flag

## Evidence Strength
Mixed — cluster-level evidence supports the interaction thesis (successful industries concentrate), but the model is hard to test prospectively and critics note successful firms thrive in "weak-diamond" home bases; treat it as a structured lens for location hypotheses, then verify with market data.

## Source
Michael Porter, *The Competitive Advantage of Nations* (1990).
