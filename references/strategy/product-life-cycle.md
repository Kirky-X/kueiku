# Product Life Cycle

## Core Concept

Products go through four stages in the market: **Introduction, Growth, Maturity, and Decline**. Each stage has different market characteristics, competitive landscape, customer composition, and profitability models, requiring different strategies and resource allocations.

> A product at different stages doesn't need "more effort" but a "different playbook." Using a growth-stage playbook for a decline-stage product is a common source of resource waste.

```
Sales Revenue
  ↑              ┌──── Maturity
  │            ╱       ╲
  │      Growth╱         ╲ Decline
  │      Stage╱             ╲
  │      ╱                 ╲
  │ Intro╱                    ╲
  └─────────────────────────────→ Time
```

| Stage | Sales Growth | Competition | Profit | Customers | Core Objective |
|------|---------|------|------|------|---------|
| Introduction | Low | Few | Loss/Break-even | Innovators | Validate PMF |
| Growth | Rapid increase | Increasing | Rising | Early adopters | Capture share |
| Maturity | Growth slowing | Maximum | Peak then declining | Early + late majority | Defend + extend |
| Decline | Decreasing | Fewer | Decreasing | Laggards | Harvest or exit |

---

## Use Cases

✅ **Best for**
- Product strategy formulation and investment timing
- Product portfolio management (product mix across different stages)
- Marketing and resource allocation decisions
- Determining whether a product needs iteration/extension/exit

⚠️ **Use with caution**
- Disruptive innovation products (irregular life cycle patterns)
- Platform/subscription products (no typical decline, need retention models)
- Using a single product life cycle to explain an entire company's fate
- Identifying stage transition points (clear in hindsight, blurry in advance)

## Execution Steps

### Step 1: Position the Product's Current Stage

Use the following signals for a holistic assessment (don't rely on a single metric):

```
Stage positioning check:
  □ Sales growth rate trend: [Accelerating/Steady/Slowing/Declining]
  □ Customer composition: [Innovators/Early adopters/Majority/Laggards proportion]
  □ Number of competitors: [Few/Increasing/Maximum/Decreasing]
  □ Profit trend: [Loss/Rising/Peak/Declining]
  □ Technology iteration speed: [Fast/Medium/Slow/Stagnant]
  □ Substitute threat: [None/Emerging/Increasing/Strong]
```

> Misidentifying the stage is the biggest risk. Mistaking maturity for growth leads to over-investment; mistaking decline for maturity causes missed exit timing.

### Step 2: Assess Stage Characteristics

For the current stage, clarify key characteristics and challenges:

```
Stage characteristics assessment (using Maturity as example):
  - Market growth: [X%] — approaching saturation
  - Competitive landscape: [Concentrated/Fragmented] — price war risk: [High/Medium/Low]
  - Customer composition: [Majority-dominated] — new customer acquisition cost: [Rising]
  - Profit pressure: [Price war/Cost increase] — profit margin: [Declining]
  - Key challenges: [Differentiation/Cost reduction/Customer retention/Category extension]
```

### Step 3: Formulate Stage Strategy

Match strategy combinations by stage:

| Stage | Marketing Strategy | Product Strategy | Investment Strategy | Organizational Strategy |
|------|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| Introduction | Educate market, focus on early users | MVP validation, rapid iteration | Precision investment, control burn rate | Small team, flat structure |
| Growth | Expand awareness, capture share | Feature expansion, performance improvement | Heavy investment for scale | Rapid hiring, build processes |
| Maturity | Defend brand, improve retention | Differentiation, cost reduction, category extension | Control investment, optimize ROI | Cost efficiency, lean operations |
| Decline | Maintain minimum investment | Maintenance-focused, no additional investment | Harvest cash | Downsize, reassign |

### Step 4: Monitor Stage Transition Signals

Set early warning indicators for stage transitions to prepare strategy shifts in advance:

```
Transition warning indicators:
  Growth → Maturity:
    □ Growth rate slowing for [N] consecutive quarters
    □ Top player share stabilizing, price wars intensifying
    □ New customer cost approaching existing customer LTV
  Maturity → Decline:
    □ Absolute sales volume declining
    □ Substitute market share rising rapidly
    □ Core customer base starting to churn
```

> Transition points often only become clear in hindsight. Setting warning indicators + quarterly reviews can buy 1-2 quarters of lead time.

## Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | How to Avoid |
|------|---------|
| Using a single metric to judge stage | Synthesize signals from sales/competition/customers/profits |
| Continuing growth-stage playbook in maturity | Proactively switch strategy mix from share capture to defense + extension |
| Fearing to exit during decline | Set clear exit thresholds and execute when reached |
| Mistaking short-term fluctuations for stage transitions | Look at 2-3 consecutive quarter trends, not single data points |
| Ignoring the portfolio perspective | A single product's decline isn't scary; what's scary is having no successor |
| Treating life cycle as fatalism | Proactive innovation (category extension/repositioning) can restart the cycle |

## Relationship with Other Methodologies

- **Combined with BCG Matrix**: BCG's stars/cash cows/question marks/dogs correspond to different life cycle stages
- **Combined with Technology Adoption Lifecycle**: Life cycle looks at products; technology adoption looks at customer segment distribution
- **Combined with Ansoff Matrix**: When seeking growth during maturity, use Ansoff to determine extension direction
- **Combined with Value Chain Analysis**: Stage transitions often accompany value chain restructuring
- **Followed by OKR**: Stage strategies are translated into corresponding cycle OKRs
