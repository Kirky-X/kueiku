# Product Life Cycle

## Core Concept
4-stage strategy matching: Introduction → Growth → Maturity → Decline.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Product strategy, investment timing, portfolio management

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- The stage diagnosis is uncertain — strategies are stage-specific, so a wrong stage call outputs the wrong playbook entirely
- Products with restart dynamics (platforms, fashion cycles, pivot-heavy startups) — the linear S-curve doesn't hold
- Fine-grained feature decisions — the unit of analysis is the product/market, not a feature

## Key Steps
1. Determine which stage each product is in
2. Apply stage-appropriate strategy:
   - Introduction: awareness, early adopters
   - Growth: market share, differentiation
   - Maturity: efficiency, defend share
   - Decline: harvest, divest, or reinvent
3. Plan transitions between stages

## Output Template

```
| Product | Stage evidence (growth rate, adoption %, price pressure) | Stage call | Stage strategy          | Transition plan            |
| ------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- | ----------------------- | -------------------------- |
| [A]     | [adoption 8%, +40%/qtr]                                  | Growth     | [share capture, channels]| [maturity signals to watch: CAC up, churn flattens] |
| [B]     | [flat sales, price wars]                                 | Maturity   | [efficiency, retention]  | [decline triggers: ...]    |

Reinvestment choice for [declining product]: harvest (cut spend, keep cash) / divest (sell, kill) / reinvent ([new segment or form])
Stage-boundary metrics: [adoption % vs category potential, sales growth, margin trend]
```

## Failure Modes
- Stage called by revenue alone: a rising line can be early adoption or peak-before-decline → triangulate adoption % of the addressable market, price pressure, and competitor exits
- Stage-mismatched KPIs: demanding efficiency from an Introduction product (or awareness from a Mature one) → change the scorecard when the stage changes
- Decline treated as failure: harvest can be the value-maximizing play → evaluate explicitly against reinvention cost

## Evidence Strength
Practitioner consensus — the pattern is widely observed at the category level, but stage duration and shape vary enormously across industries (some products skip or loop stages); use the stage call as a strategy-matching hypothesis verified by market data, not a forecast.

## Source
Theodore Levitt (1965); product life cycle theory.
