# Can't-Won't Defensibility

## Core Concept
Competitor can't or won't replicate your advantage. Moat strategies based on structural or will-based barriers.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Investor questions about competition, moat design

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- Early exploration with no traction yet — most "moats" claimed pre-launch are wishes; use the framework to rank hypotheses, not to conclude
- The advantage is a feature or a price point — those are copied in weeks; the framework is for structural positions
- You only need a competitor response forecast, not a moat audit (use Competitive Battlecard instead)

## Key Steps
1. List your competitive advantages
2. For each, classify: Can't (structural — competitor physically can't copy) vs Won't (competitor chooses not to because it conflicts with their model)
3. Strengthen Can't-Won't advantages
4. Be honest about copyable advantages

## Output Template

```
| Advantage              | Can't / Won't / Copyable | Why a top-3 competitor can't or won't          | Halflife if copied | Strengthen by        |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------ | -------------------- |
| [proprietary dataset]  | Can't                    | [years of accumulation, no external source]     | [~3 yrs]           | [exclusive feeds]    |
| [SMB-first pricing]    | Won't                    | [incumbent's enterprise sales model can't go down] | [~18 months]    | [lock in segment]    |
| [nicer UI]             | Copyable                 | —                                              | [weeks]            | [don't bet on it]    |

Honest summary: our durable moat is [X]; [Y] buys time only; [Z] is not a moat.
```

## Failure Modes
- Confusing "hasn't yet" with "can't" → name the specific mechanism (patent, data accumulation, unit-economics conflict) or classify as copyable
- Won't barriers assumed permanent: incumbents pivot when the prize grows big enough → attach a halflife and a tripwire
- Moat talk replacing momentum: early teams spend more time claiming moats than building them → cap this analysis at one page until there is traction to defend

## Evidence Strength
Practitioner consensus — the Can't/Won't taxonomy (from the 7 Powers tradition) is a widely used lens among investors and strategists; persistence of specific moats varies hugely by industry and is an empirical question per case, not something the framework settles.

## Source
Hamilton Helmer, *7 Powers* (2016); moat strategy framework.
