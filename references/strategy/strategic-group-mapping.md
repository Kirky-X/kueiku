# Strategic Group Mapping

## Core Concept
Map companies within an industry by strategic similarity. Identify clusters and mobility barriers between groups.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Industry competitive structure analysis, new entrant positioning, strategic differentiation

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- Industry with < ~8 heterogeneous players — groups need enough members to be meaningful
- All competitors converge on one strategy (commodity) — there is one group; the map is empty
- The strategic dimensions that matter can't be observed (private companies, opaque pricing)

## Key Steps
1. Identify key strategic dimensions (e.g. price/quality, geographic scope, vertical integration)
2. Plot all industry players on 2D map
3. Identify clusters (strategic groups)
4. Assess mobility barriers between groups
5. Identify your position and whitespace opportunities

## Output Template

```
Dimensions: x = [price tier], y = [channel model]

  high ┤  [Group A: premium direct — Firm1, Firm2]
       │            ↑ mobility barrier: [brand + field org]
       │  [our position ★]
       │  [Group B: value retail — Firm3, Firm4, Firm5]
  low  ┤  [Group C: regional niche — Firm6, Firm7]
       └──────────────────────────────
Group performance notes: [avg margin / growth per group, where observable]
Whitespace: [position with demand evidence but no incumbent] — why empty: [barrier or no demand?]
Entry target group & required barriers to cross: [list]
```

## Failure Modes
- Arbitrary dimensions: axes picked for visual convenience produce groups that don't behave alike → test alternative dimension pairs; keep the one where within-group performance clusters
- Whitespace romanticized: an empty map region may be empty because it's unprofitable → demand-side evidence before calling it opportunity
- Barrier blindness: planning a group jump while ignoring what pins incumbents in (assets, contracts, identity) → list the barrier, then the plan to cross or nullify it

## Evidence Strength
Practitioner consensus — the concept of intra-industry strategic clusters is well established in strategy research; precise group boundaries are analyst-dependent, and profitability differences between groups are an empirical question per industry rather than a given.
