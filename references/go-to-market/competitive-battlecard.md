# Competitive Battlecard

## Core Concept
A one-page sales enablement tool with 4 sections: Where We Win / Where They Win / Common Objections & Responses / Landmines (traps to set for competitors). Updated quarterly based on win/loss data.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Sales competitive response
- Win/loss pattern analysis
- Competitive positioning

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- Fewer than ~10 competitive deals in CRM — the patterns are anecdotes; interview reps qualitatively instead
- The category has no real competitor (new category creation) — landmines and objections target named rivals; use Positioning Strategy
- As a strategy substitute: battlecards encode the current competitive position; they cannot decide it (route to Porter's Five Forces for structure)

## Key Steps
1. Gather win/loss data from CRM and sales interviews
2. Identify top 3-5 competitors
3. For each competitor, fill 4 sections: Where We Win / Where They Win / Objections & Responses / Landmines
4. Review with sales team; validate accuracy
5. Distribute and train sales team
6. Update quarterly with new competitive intelligence

## Output Template

```
Competitor: [name] — deal overlap: [segments where you meet]

WHERE WE WIN:  [conditions + deal evidence]
WHERE THEY WIN: [conditions + deal evidence — honest, or the card gets ignored]
OBJECTIONS & RESPONSES:
  "[objection]" → [response + proof point]
LANDMINES: [question to plant that surfaces their weakness honestly]
Refresh: [date + data source]  Owner: [...]
```

## Failure Modes
- Propaganda card: "we win everywhere" — reps stop trusting the card → the WHERE THEY WIN section must be honest enough to be useful
- Set-and-forget: card drifts behind the competitor's current release → quarterly refresh with a named owner, triggered also by any lost-deal pattern shift
- Landmines as FUD: questions that mislead rather than surface truth → a landmine must be a question you would want answered honestly as the buyer

## Evidence Strength
Practitioner consensus in sales enablement; win/loss-informed cards are standard practice with consistent anecdotal reports of improved competitive win rates. No controlled studies isolate the card's effect from rep quality; the 4-section structure is convention, not a validated model.

## Source
Standard sales enablement methodology; competitive intelligence frameworks.
