# Positioning Strategy

## Core Idea
Positioning is not about "what we do" but about "which position we occupy in the user's mind." By comparing 5 positioning angles against the top 5 competitors and proactively seeking "white space" (positions competitors are unwilling or unable to occupy), your product becomes naturally recalled when users make a choice.

## Applicable Scenarios
- Users can't distinguish you from competitors
- Sales compete on price rather than positioning
- Product has expanded in too many directions, making the positioning unclear

## Key Steps
1. List 5 positioning angles:
   - Analogy ("the X for Y industry")
   - Feature leadership ("the fastest / most secure / cheapest")
   - Scenario-specific ("purpose-built for [scenario]")
   - Reverse positioning ("not X, but Y")
   - Category creation ("a whole new category, nothing to compare to")
2. List the top 5 direct competitors and note the position each currently occupies
3. Draw a 5×5 matrix: angles × competitors, find the "white space" cells — positions competitors haven't claimed but you can
4. Select one primary positioning (choose only one to avoid fragmented mindshare)
5. Validation: have 10 target users repeat your positioning back to you; if it matches what you designed, it holds
6. Consistent communication: website / sales scripts / ads / onboarding all aligned to the chosen positioning

## Source
April Dunford, *Obviously Awesome* (2019); positioning theory originated in Al Ries & Jack Trout, *Positioning: The Battle for Your Mind*
