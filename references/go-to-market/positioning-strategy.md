# Positioning Strategy

## Core Concept
Find whitespace in user minds by analyzing 5 classic positioning bases (Kotler-style) × top-5 competitor matrix. The 5 angles: Features/Benefits, Use Case/Application, User Category, Price/Quality, Competitor Comparison. Identify where competitors cluster and find the gap.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- User mindshare positioning
- Differentiated positioning
- Brand strategy

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- Nobody holds the "position" being contested yet (new category) — a matrix of empty cells is noise; define the category first
- Positioning is not the constraint (distribution, product, or pricing is broken) — repositioning relabels a problem it cannot fix
- No access to customer perception data — the map must reflect minds, not internal slide decks; gather perception evidence first

## Key Steps
1. List top 5 competitors
2. For each competitor, map their current positioning across 5 angles
3. Create a positioning matrix showing competitor clusters
4. Identify whitespace: angles where no competitor is positioned
5. Select your positioning angle based on: whitespace + your strength + customer need
6. Craft positioning statement: For [target] who [need], [product] is a [category] that [benefit]. Unlike [competitor], we [differentiator].

## Output Template

```
Competitor × angle matrix (5 competitors × 5 angles): [table/map]

Clusters: [where competitors bunch and why]
Whitespace candidates: [angle + why it is open + why anyone cares]
Selected angle: [angle] — backed by [our strength] × [customer need]

Positioning statement: For [target] who [need], [product] is a [category]
that [benefit]. Unlike [competitor], we [differentiator].
Validation: [how perception will be re-measured and when]
```

## Failure Modes
- Whitespace for whitespace's sake: an empty cell nobody wants → every whitespace candidate must pass "a customer cares" before selection
- Aspirational mapping: the map reflects how we wish the market saw competitors → source each competitor's angle from their own messaging and customer quotes
- Statement without sacrifice: positioning that excludes nothing sticks nowhere → the statement must name what you are not

## Evidence Strength
Practitioner consensus from brand strategy; mindshare positioning theory traces to Ries & Trout, the 5-angle matrix to classic (Kotler-style) positioning bases, and the statement template to Geoffrey Moore. None of the three pieces is an empirically validated model, and mindshare causality is hard to measure — map quality depends entirely on the quality of perception data gathered.

## Source
Al Ries & Jack Trout, *Positioning: The Battle for Your Mind* (1981) — mindshare theory; 5 positioning bases follow classic (Kotler-style) practice; positioning-statement template follows Geoffrey Moore, *Crossing the Chasm* (1991).
