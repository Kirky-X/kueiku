# Beachhead Segment

## Core Concept
Select your initial market segment using 4 criteria: Burning Pain (must-have), Willingness to Pay, Winnable (60-70% win rate), Referral potential. Concentrate all resources on winning one segment before expanding — "crossing the chasm."

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Crossing the chasm (early market entry)
- Concentrating resources on a winnable segment
- B2B market segmentation

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- The market is a single homogeneous segment — there is nothing to concentrate; go straight to GTM Strategy
- No segment data exists yet (no win rates, no WTP signals) — the 4 criteria become guesses; run cheap validation experiments first
- Platform/marketplace businesses where the "segment" is defined by network effects rather than customer attributes — the concentration logic differs

## Key Steps
1. List all potential market segments
2. Evaluate each against 4 criteria: Burning Pain / WTP / Winnable 60-70% / Referral
3. Score and rank segments
4. Select the top segment as beachhead
5. Develop go-to-market plan focused exclusively on this segment
6. Win the beachhead before expanding to adjacent segments

## Output Template

```
Segments considered: [list, one line each]

Scoring (1-5 per criterion: Burning Pain / WTP / Winnable / Referral):
  [Segment A]  4 / 4 / 4 / 3  → 15  ← beachhead
  [Segment B]  3 / 5 / 2 / 2  → 12

Beachhead: [segment] — why winnable: [evidence behind the 60-70% claim]
Explicitly deferred: [B, C] — what would reopen them
Beachhead plan: [channel + first 10 target accounts/communities]
```

## Failure Modes
- Scoring theater: segments scored from enthusiasm instead of evidence → require one observable fact per criterion before a score counts
- Beachhead drift: "winning" loosely, then expanding with nothing won → define "won" (e.g. 3 referenceable customers) before starting
- Wrong-unit segments: segments defined by product features instead of customer situations → re-express each segment as "people who hire X for job Y"

## Evidence Strength
Practitioner consensus from B2B go-to-market practice (Moore's chasm model); the 60-70% winnability figure is a heuristic, not an empirical constant. Survivor bias applies — beachhead successes get told more often than beachhead dead-ends.

## Source
Geoffrey Moore, *Crossing the Chasm* (1991) — beachhead/chasm strategy; the 4-criteria scorecard is practitioner convention (cf. Bill Aulet, *Disciplined Entrepreneurship*, beachhead selection).
