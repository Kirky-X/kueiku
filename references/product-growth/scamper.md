# SCAMPER

## Core Concept
7-dimension creative trigger for systematic innovation: Substitute / Combine / Adapt / Modify (Magnify) / Put to other use / Eliminate / Reverse. Forces thinking about existing products from 7 different angles.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Product iteration
- Experience optimization
- Systematic innovation workshops

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- Zero-based innovation — SCAMPER iterates an existing artifact; from-scratch concepts need open generative methods, not 7 angles on what exists
- Unvalidated idea generation — SCAMPER yields quantity, not quality; without screening it feeds the backlog with gimmicks
- Substitute for diagnosis — pushing modification angles at a misidentified job produces polished wrongness; nail the job with **JTBD** first
- Strategy setting — which dimension to push should follow a positioning insight, not mechanical traversal of the letters

## Key Steps
1. Take the existing product/service/process as the target
2. Apply each SCAMPER dimension:
   - **S**ubstitute: what component can be replaced?
   - **C**ombine: what can be merged?
   - **A**dapt: what can be borrowed from elsewhere?
   - **M**odify/Magnify: what can be exaggerated or changed?
   - **P**ut to other use: who else could use this?
   - **E**liminate: what can be removed?
   - **R**everse/Rearrange: what can be reordered?
3. Evaluate generated ideas for feasibility and impact
4. Select top ideas for prototyping

## Output Template

```
Target: [product/service/process + current state in one line]
S Substitute: [component → replacement]
C Combine: [what merges with what]
A Adapt: [borrowed from which context]
M Modify/Magnify: [what scales, shrinks, or changes shape]
P Put to other use: [new user / new scenario]
E Eliminate: [what gets removed + what risk that carries]
R Reverse/Rearrange: [what sequence or layout inverts]
Screen: [top ideas by impact × feasibility → prototype candidates]
```

## Failure Modes
- Mechanical traversal: forcing one idea per letter produces filler → treat the dimensions as triggers; linger where they spark and allow zero ideas for some letters
- Quantity worship: 40 ideas presented as the deliverable with no screening → always close with impact × feasibility screening; ideation is half the work, evaluation is the other half
- Anchor blindness: 7 angles on the same artifact still anchor to it → occasionally pair with a from-scratch prompt ("if this didn't exist, what would we build?")
- Novelty bias: weirder ideas feel more creative in the room → judge ideas by evidence of user value, not session energy

## Evidence Strength
Practitioner consensus — a widely used ideation heuristic with no formal validity studies; its value is as a structured prompt set that reduces blank-page paralysis and forces angles teams skip, not as a validated predictor of which ideas will succeed.
