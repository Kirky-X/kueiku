# JTBD — Jobs To Be Done

## Core Concept
Users "hire" products to get a "job" done. The job is not the product — it's the progress the user wants to make in a specific situation. Focus on the job, not the customer attributes.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Demand mining
- Product positioning
- Competitive replacement analysis

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- Pure usability refinement of a shipped flow — the job is settled; you need usability testing, not switch interviews
- Aesthetic/emotional products where the "functional job" frame flattens what matters — stretch to social/emotional jobs deliberately or the analysis misses
- Jobs invented from internal brainstorming — JTBD without the switch interview is personas with new vocabulary

## Key Steps
1. Conduct "switch interview": ask about the moment they decided to hire/fire a product
2. Identify the job statement: "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]"
3. Classify the job: functional / emotional / social
4. Map the job process (8 steps: define → locate → prepare → confirm → execute → monitor → modify → complete)
5. Identify pain points and opportunities in the job process

## Output Template

```
Switch story: [who, from what → to what, when, trigger event]
  Push of the old: [what made the old solution intolerable]
  Pull of the new: [what attracted]
  Anxiety: [what almost stopped the switch]
  Habit: [what nearly kept them]

Job statement: When [I'm onboarding a new analyst], I want to [get our reporting consistent fast], so I can [trust the numbers by Friday]
  Functional: [consistent reports] Emotional: [confidence] Social: [look competent to leadership]

Job map pain points: [biggest friction steps with evidence]
Competition by job: [spreadsheet / agency / doing nothing — all compete for the same job]
Opportunity: [underserved step + what serving it would look like]
```

## Failure Modes
- Product-shaped jobs: "when I want to create a pivot table" — that's a feature, not a job → jobs survive product categories; write them so a non-customer could read them and nod
- Single-interview certainty: one vivid switch story becomes strategy → look for the pattern across switches (and fires); n of 1 is anecdote
- Ignoring the fire side: why customers leave teaches as much as why they buy → interview recent quitters, not just recent adopters

## Evidence Strength
Practitioner consensus — switch interviewing and job statements are widely credited for demand insight and stable positioning (jobs outlive products); the theory's stronger causal claims (jobs fully predict adoption) remain debated in the innovation literature, and interview-based methods inherit small-n and recall-bias limits.

## Source
Clayton Christensen, *The Innovator's Solution* (2003); Anthony Ulwick, *What Customers Want*.
