# Market Segmentation

## Core Concept
Divide the market into 3-5 non-overlapping segments based on behavior + JTBD + needs. Each segment should be distinct, measurable, accessible, and actionable. Done early in strategy to clarify who you serve.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Early strategy: defining who to serve
- Resource allocation across segments
- Positioning differentiation

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- Segmenting an installed base you can already observe — that's profiling current users; **User Segmentation** or **RFM Model** on behavior data beats survey clusters
- Variables with no link to the buying decision — demographic clusters that buy identically are fiction; start from **JTBD** and needs, let variables follow
- One-off clustering presented as durable truth — markets shift; segments are hypotheses that need re-validation
- Picking where to compete — the map says who exists, not who to serve; pair with sizing and willingness-to-pay evidence before targeting

## Key Steps
1. List potential segmentation variables (behavior, demographics, JTBD, needs)
2. Cluster customers into 3-5 segments using the variables
3. Validate: are segments non-overlapping, measurable, accessible, and sizable?
4. For each segment, define: size, growth, willingness to pay, competitive intensity
5. Prioritize segments for targeting
6. Develop segment-specific value propositions

## Output Template

```
Segment: [name]
  Definition: [cluster variables + one-line description]
  Size: [# accounts or % of market]  Growth: [trend]
  Distinct job: [what they hire the product for]
  Willingness to pay: [range]
  Accessibility: [how you reach them]
  Competitive intensity: [who else serves them]
Fit: [why the current offer wins/loses here]
Priority: [rank + evidence]
```

## Failure Modes
- Demographic clustering of a behavioral problem: segments split by age or title that buy identically → cluster on needs and behavior first; keep demographics only where they demonstrably track the decision
- Brainstormed segments dressed as research: clusters invented in a workshop → ground every segment in observed data and name the evidence per attribute
- Unactionable segments: 8 clusters with no size or reach data → apply the distinct/measurable/accessible/actionable test; merge or drop what fails
- Frozen segmentation: last year's deck still drives targeting → set a re-validation trigger (market shift, declining response rates, new entrant)

## Evidence Strength
Practitioner consensus — STP is foundational marketing doctrine and segmentation reliably improves targeting efficiency, but the validity of any given segmentation depends entirely on whether the clustering variables track the actual buying decision, and segments decay as markets shift; treat outputs as hypotheses to validate, not facts.
