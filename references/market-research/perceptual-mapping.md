# Perceptual Mapping

## Core Concept
2D coordinate chart showing how consumers perceive brands/products on two key dimensions. Reveals competitive positioning, whitespace opportunities, and potential repositioning needs.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Brand positioning analysis
- Competitive perception comparison
- Positioning adjustment decisions

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- Dimensions nobody validated — a 2D map hides whichever axis actually drives choice; confirm axis importance with **Kano** or driver analysis before plotting
- Reading preference into perception — the map shows how brands are seen, not what buyers want; the "empty corner" may be empty because nobody wants to live there
- B2B markets with a handful of accounts — averaging 50+ respondent ratings presumes a large homogeneous pool; analyze key accounts directly instead
- Deciding repositioning from the map alone — it says where you are, not whether you can get there; pair with a capability and cost assessment

## Key Steps
1. Select 2 key dimensions that matter to consumers (e.g. price vs quality, traditional vs modern)
2. Survey 50+ target segment users: rate each brand on both dimensions (1-10)
3. Plot average ratings on 2D chart
4. Analyze: where do competitors cluster? Where is whitespace?
5. Assess: is your current position where you want to be?
6. If repositioning needed, plan messaging/product changes to shift perception

## Output Template

```
Dimensions: X = [low ↔ high price]  Y = [traditional ↔ modern] (validated as decision drivers: [evidence])
Respondents: [n] from [segment], mapped on [date]
Positions (avg rating 1-10): [Brand A]: ([x,y])  [Brand B]: ([x,y])  [Us]: ([x,y])
Clusters: [brands competing on the same territory]
Whitespace: [empty position — and why: unmet demand or no demand]
Gap: current ([x,y]) → target ([x,y]); shift levers: [product / messaging changes]
```

## Failure Modes
- Arbitrary axes: dimensions chosen for chart neatness rather than decision influence → validate axes against stated-importance or driver data; an unimportant dimension makes an unimportant map
- Empty-quadrant fallacy: treating any gap as opportunity → whitespace is only opportunity if someone wants to occupy it; test willingness to pay before calling it
- Perception ≠ preference ≠ feasibility: a desirable position you cannot deliver erodes trust → confirm deliverability before investing in repositioning
- Snapshot staleness: perception moves with every campaign and competitor launch → date the map and set a re-measurement cadence; never re-run strategy on an old chart

## Evidence Strength
Practitioner consensus — perceptual mapping is standard marketing practice with strong face validity for visualizing relative brand positions; its weakness is that the output is only as valid as the dimension choice and rating data behind it, and it captures perception at a point in time rather than causally explaining choice.

## Source
Standard marketing research methodology; multivariate analysis technique.
