# Technology Adoption Lifecycle

## Core Concept
5-stage model of technology adoption: Innovators (2.5%) → Early Adopters (13.5%) → Early Majority (34%) → Late Majority (34%) → Laggards (16%). The "chasm" between Early Adopters and Early Majority is where most technology products fail.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Technology product marketing
- Crossing the chasm strategy
- Target customer segment selection

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- Products whose buyers aren't technology-attitude-driven — the bell-curve psychology was derived from technology adoption, not all purchasing
- Applying the percentages as forecasts — they're a stylized description of diffusion, not your funnel's numbers
- Category-erasing products (smartphones) that collapsed stages — don't force chasm logic onto products riding an existing behavior

## Key Steps
1. Assess current customer composition across 5 adopter categories
2. Identify which stage your product is in
3. If at the chasm (between Early Adopters and Early Majority): develop a whole product solution for a specific beachhead segment
4. Adjust marketing messaging per adopter type
5. Plan strategies to cross the chasm

## Output Template

```
Customer composition: innovators [n (%)] / early adopters [n (%)] / early majority [n (%)] / later [n (%)]

Stage call: [early adopters] — evidence: [references-driven sales, tolerance for gaps, vision purchases]

Chasm check: 
  Early-adopter sales: [growing/plateauing]
  Early-majority objections heard: ["who else like us uses it?", "where's the support/integrations?"] — these are the chasm's voice
  Beachhead candidate: [one segment, narrowly defined] — whole-product gaps to close for them: [support, integrations, references]

Message shift: vision talk (adopters) → [segment-specific whole-product proof] (majority)
Chasm plan: [narrow the target → complete the whole product → win references → expand to adjacent segments]
```

## Failure Modes
- Chasm invoked for every slowdown: plateau may be product, pricing, or channel — not adoption psychology → verify with buyer evidence (reference requests, whole-product complaints) before prescribing chasm strategy
- Beachhead skipped: "cross the chasm" used to justify broadening marketing — the opposite of the prescription → the strategy is concentration: one segment, complete product, dominant reference
- Percentages treated as data: reporting "we've captured 13.5%" — the curve describes adopter types, not a market-share forecast → diagnose which type your current customers behave like

## Evidence Strength
Mixed — diffusion theory (Rogers) is well established sociologically; Moore's chasm prescriptions are influential practitioner lore with memorable case support but limited systematic validation, and the adopter percentages are heuristics from older diffusion studies rather than parameters of modern markets.

## Source
Everett Rogers, *Diffusion of Innovations* (1962); Geoffrey Moore, *Crossing the Chasm* (1991).
