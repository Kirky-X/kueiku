# User Personas

## Core Concept
Create 3 representative user personas, each containing: JTBD (what job they hire the product for), Top 3 Pains, Top 3 Gains, and Unexpected Insights from research. Used to align team understanding of target users.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Team alignment on target users
- Design decisions
- Feature prioritization

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- Built from imagination or a single stakeholder's opinion — fictional personas launder assumptions as research
- Marketing segments posing as personas: "Marketing Mary" demographics with no job, pains, or behavioral basis → segments describe who buys; personas should describe who uses and why
- Small user bases where you can just talk to actual users — a persona of your 40 customers is worse than their names

## Key Steps
1. Conduct 8-12 user interviews (or analyze survey data)
2. Cluster interview findings by behavior patterns and needs
3. Create 3 personas, each with: Name, Photo, Demographics, JTBD, Top 3 Pains, Top 3 Gains, Unexpected Insight
4. Validate personas with additional data
5. Use personas in design reviews and prioritization discussions

## Output Template

```
Persona: [Name] — [role, context] — research base: [n interviews, dates]

JTBD: When [situation], they need [job], so they can [outcome]
Top 3 Pains: 1. [with evidence quote] 2. [...] 3. [...]
Top 3 Gains: 1. [...] 2. [...] 3. [...]
Unexpected insight: [what surprised the team — the reason this persona earns its page]
Behavioral anchors: [tools used today, workaround in place, spending/sunk time]

Persona in use: design review cites [which persona + which pain]; feature [X] rejected because no persona's job matches
Expiry: re-validate when [new segment / major behavior shift / 12 months]
```

## Failure Modes
- Demographic filler: personas describing age and coffee habits but no job or pain → every line must trace to interview evidence; cut anything that doesn't
- Persona worship: designing for "Sarah" instead of for the evidence Sarah represents → personas are communication devices for clustered data; when they conflict with new data, data wins
- Never retired: personas from 3 years ago still steering decisions → date the research base and set a re-validation trigger; stale personas are confident lies

## Evidence Strength
Practitioner consensus — research-backed personas (grounded in real interviews and behavioral clustering) are widely valued for team alignment; the method has documented weaknesses too (confirmation bias in clustering, over-literal design for archetypes), and evidence that personas improve design outcomes comes mainly from practitioner reports rather than controlled studies.
