# Principled Negotiation (Harvard Method)

## Core Concept
Negotiate over interests, not positions, and derive your walk-away power from alternatives, not aggression. Four principles: separate the people from the problem; bargain over interests behind stated positions; invent options for mutual gain before dividing; insist on objective criteria. BATNA — your Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement — is the load-bearing element: value your best fallback before the table, set the reservation point from it, and estimate the counterpart's BATNA to know where the zone of agreement actually sits.

## Applicable Scenarios
✅ **Best for**
- Salary, vendor, partnership, licensing negotiations
- Resource disputes and multi-round agreements where the relationship survives the deal
- Deadlocked positional bargaining that needs reframing

⚠️ **When NOT to use**
- No acceptable alternative exists — build the BATNA first (alternatives, option value via Reversibility Grading + Decision Matrix); negotiating without one is theater. Escalation: mapping who influences the outcome without a live counterpart → Stakeholder Mapping (Strategic Analysis)
- Pure one-shot price haggling against a professional positional counterpart — leverage math and anchoring dominate; principles add little
- Values or identity conflicts disguised as interest disputes — no clever package dissolves them; escalate or restructure the relationship instead
- The counterpart dictates terms unilaterally — that is decision-making under constraint, not negotiation

## Key Steps
1. Prepare interests: list yours and the counterpart's likely interests behind their stated position ("why" behind each demand)
2. Build BATNA: enumerate alternatives, value the best one concretely, set the reservation point (walk-away) from it; estimate their BATNA the same way
3. Run two tracks: relationship (soft) on one, substance (hard) on the other — never trade one for the other
4. Negotiate interests: restate the position, ask the why, propose against the underlying interest
5. Invent options before committing: expand the pie (scheduling, scope, risk-sharing, sequencing) before splitting it
6. Insist on objective criteria: market benchmarks, industry standards, third-party appraisals as the fairness basis
7. Close or walk: written agreement against the criteria, or exercise the BATNA — a prepared walk-away is the strongest move in the room

## Output Template

```
Parties: [us / them / affected third parties]
Interests — ours: [listed]; theirs: [stated + inferred, tagged fact/inference]
BATNA — ours: [best alternative + valued reservation point]; theirs: [estimate + confidence]
Zone of agreement: [overlap of reservation points, or none]
Options for mutual gain: [packages varying scope/timing/risk-sharing]
Objective criteria: [benchmarks both sides would accept]
Concession plan: [what we give cheaply, what we hold, in what order]
Walk-away trigger: [the condition that ends talks]
```

## Failure Modes
- Positions-first bargaining: countering their number hardens identities → force the "why" question before any counteroffer
- Reservation point anchored on aspiration instead of BATNA: the walk-away must come from alternatives valued outside the room, or you either overhold or capitulate
- BATNA hallucination: an alternative that "sounds available" but would not survive scrutiny → pressure-test it before relying on it
- Winning the deal, losing the relationship (or relationship-smoothing that gives away substance): the two tracks must stay separate by design
- Revealing the reservation point through eagerness: sequence concessions deliberately, never bid against yourself

## Evidence Strength
Practitioner consensus with a strong research core — BATNA/reservation-point effects are among the most replicated findings in negotiation research; the "invent options for mutual gain" prescriptions transfer less reliably to adversarial one-shot settings and remain debated there.
